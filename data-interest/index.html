Eight charts that made me say, wait, what?

I like data because it can make normal news feel weird in a good way. These are short, chartable stories from the last few years where the numbers were louder than the headline.

AI

2023-2026

ChatGPT Went From Nerd Toy To Group-Project Teammate

18%2023 23%2024 34%2025 44%2026

In 2023, ChatGPT still felt like something one kid in class knew about and would not stop explaining. By 2026, Pew found that 44% of U.S. adults had used ChatGPT or another chatbot. That is a pretty wild jump. The funny part is that people still ask if AI is a fad while almost half the country has already tried it. My favorite chart idea here is just a staircase: 18, 23, 34, 44. Simple. Slightly scary. Very "my English teacher has opinions now."

Source: Pew Research Center

Health

2024-2025

GLP-1 Drugs Became The Main Character

6%2024 12%2025

GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound went from medical news to lunch-table conversation really fast. KFF found that current use among U.S. adults doubled from 6% in 2024 to 12% in late 2025. The FDA also said semaglutide products had been in shortage since 2022 because demand got so high. That is when you know a trend is not just online noise. It was affecting doctors, pharmacies, insurance, and probably a lot of awkward family group chats.

Sources: KFF, FDA

Work

2022-2025

Return-To-Office Was Loud, But Remote Work Stayed

1.60d2022 1.33d2023 1.27d2024/25

Every few months, there is another dramatic headline about everyone going back to the office. But the data is more like, "cool story, anyway." Stanford researchers found that working from home dropped after 2022, then basically stabilized. Among surveyed college-educated workers, it went from about 1.6 days a week in 2022 to 1.27 days in late 2024/early 2025. So remote work did not disappear. It just became normal and a little less exciting, which is honestly how most adult things seem to end.

Source: Stanford SIEPR

Transportation

2025

The EV Slowdown Was Mostly An American Plot Twist

25%World 55%China <10%U.S.

If you only follow U.S. car news, EVs can sound like they hit a wall. Globally, though, electric cars were still zooming. The IEA said more than 20 million electric cars were sold in 2025, about one in four new cars worldwide. China was on another level, with electric cars close to 55% of new sales. The U.S. was just under 10%. So the story is not "EVs are over." It is more like the world pressed fast-forward and America was still searching for the charger.

Source: IEA Global EV Outlook

Energy

2022-2025

Solar Power Had A Massive Glow-Up

~1,3002022 TWh ~2,7002025 TWh

Solar used to feel like the future. In 2025, it started feeling more like the present forgot to send a calendar invite. The IEA says global solar generation reached nearly 2,700 TWh in 2025, more than double its 2022 output. Solar also added around 600 TWh in one year, which the IEA called the largest one-year electricity generation increase for any source outside weird rebound years. Translation: solar did not just grow. It sprinted, stretched, and asked coal if it needed a water break.

Source: IEA Global Energy Review

Money + Sports

2021-2025

Sports Betting Became Way Too Easy To Find

$57B2021 $120B2023 $167B2025

Sports betting went from "Vegas thing" to "why is there an odds button next to every game?" The American Gaming Association reported $57.2 billion in legal sports betting handle in 2021. By 2025, that number was $166.9 billion. That is almost triple in four years. I get why this is a huge business: sports already make people extremely confident about things they cannot control. But the chart also has a serious side. When betting becomes this normal, responsible gambling cannot be an afterthought.

Sources: AGA 2021, AGA 2025

Sports

2020-2025

Pickleball Escaped And Now Everyone Has A Paddle

4.2M2020 24.3M2025

Pickleball might be the funniest growth chart in sports. SFIA says U.S. participation went from about 4.2 million players in 2020 to 24.3 million in 2025. That is not a trend. That is a takeover with plastic balls. The best part is that the player base also got younger, so the old stereotype is not really accurate anymore. Pickleball found the perfect formula: easy to start, social, competitive enough, and just goofy enough that nobody feels too serious until suddenly everyone is very serious.

Source: SFIA

Sports Media

2023-2024

Women's Basketball Did Not Have A Moment. It Had Receipts.

6,6152023 9,8072024

In 2024, women's basketball basically walked into the ratings conversation and took a chair. ESPN said the NCAA women's championship drew 18.9 million viewers, the biggest women's college basketball audience ever. The WNBA also had its best regular-season viewership in 24 years and its highest attendance in 22 years. Average attendance rose to 9,807 fans per game, up 48% from 2023. So no, this was not just "people were curious." The numbers say fans showed up, watched, bought merch, and made the bandwagon get a second bandwagon.

Sources: ESPN, WNBA
